🧰 First Aid Instructions

  • Burns 🔥
    1. Minor Burns (First-Degree): Run cool (not cold) water over the burn for 10–15 minutes or apply a cold, wet compress. Avoid ice, as it can cause further damage. Gently pat the burn dry with a clean cloth.
    2. Moderate Burns (Second-Degree): Cool the burn under running water or with a damp cloth. Do not pop blisters. Cover with a sterile, non-stick dressing. Seek medical help if blisters appear or the burn covers a large area.
    3. Severe Burns (Third-Degree): Call emergency services immediately. Cover the area with a clean, non-stick cloth. Do not remove clothing stuck to the skin or immerse in water. Monitor for shock (pale skin, shallow breathing, confusion).
  • Fractures 🦴
    1. Suspected Fracture: Immobilize the injured area. Use a splint (e.g., board or rolled newspaper) and secure with bandages or cloth—ensure it's not too tight.
    2. Open Fracture: Cover the wound with a sterile bandage or clean cloth. Do not attempt to push the bone back in. Keep the victim calm and seek immediate medical help.
    3. Dislocation: Do not attempt to realign the joint. Immobilize and get medical help as soon as possible.
  • Cardiac Arrest ❤️‍🔥
    1. Call Emergency Services: Dial emergency services or ask someone else to do so immediately.
    2. CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation): If unresponsive and not breathing, begin chest compressions. Place your hands in the center of the chest and push hard and fast (2 inches deep at 100–120 compressions per minute). Alternate with rescue breaths if trained.
  • Choking 😷
    1. Mild Choking (Able to Cough or Speak): Encourage coughing to dislodge the object.
    2. Severe Choking (Unable to Breathe or Talk): Perform the Heimlich maneuver. Stand behind the person, place your fist above the navel, and thrust inward and upward. If unconscious, begin CPR and check the mouth between compressions.
  • Severe Bleeding 🩸
    1. Direct Pressure: Apply firm pressure on the wound with a clean cloth. Do not remove soaked cloth—add more layers.
    2. Elevate: If possible, raise the injured limb above heart level.
    3. Pressure Points: Press major arteries (e.g., brachial for arms, femoral for legs) if bleeding continues.
    4. Tourniquet: As a last resort, apply a tourniquet above the wound. Record the time. Seek emergency help immediately.
  • Heatstroke 🥵
    1. Move to a Cool Place: Get the person into shade or an air-conditioned environment.
    2. Cool the Body: Use cool, wet cloths, fans, or immersion in cool water—especially around neck, armpits, and groin.
    3. Hydration: Offer cool water or electrolyte drinks. Avoid alcohol or caffeine.
    4. Monitor Symptoms: Watch for confusion, seizures, or unconsciousness. Call emergency services immediately if symptoms worsen.
  • Hypothermia ❄️
    1. Get to Warmth: Move the person to a warm, dry area.
    2. Remove Wet Clothing: Replace with dry, warm clothing. Be gentle while handling.
    3. Warm the Body: Offer warm liquids (no alcohol/caffeine). Use warm compresses on chest, neck, and head first.
    4. Seek Medical Help: If the person is unresponsive or severely affected, get emergency medical assistance immediately.
  • Asthma Attack 🫁
    1. Stay Calm: Encourage the person to remain calm to avoid worsening the situation.
    2. Use Inhaler: If the person has a prescribed inhaler, encourage them to use it immediately.
    3. Sit Up Straight: Help the person to sit up straight to open the airways.
    4. Monitor Breathing: If symptoms don't improve, help the person use the inhaler again after 10 minutes.
    5. Seek Emergency Help: If symptoms worsen, call emergency services immediately.
  • Seizures ⚡
    1. Protect the Person from Injury: Clear the area of sharp objects and place a cushion or cloth under the person's head.
    2. Do Not Restrain: Never try to hold the person down during a seizure.
    3. Turn the Person on Their Side: After the seizure stops, roll the person onto their side to allow fluids to drain.
    4. Time the Seizure: If it lasts more than 5 minutes, call emergency services immediately.
    5. Post-Seizure Care: Reassure and keep the person calm once the seizure ends.
  • Poisoning ☠️
    1. Assess the Situation: Determine what was ingested, how much, and when it occurred.
    2. Call Emergency Services: Immediately contact emergency services or poison control.
    3. Do Not Induce Vomiting: Never induce vomiting unless instructed by a medical professional.
    4. Keep the Person Calm: Keep the person calm and monitor their breathing.
    5. Provide Information: Give responders as much information as possible about the substance involved.
